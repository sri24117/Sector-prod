**What this actually means:** the market is real and fragmented across two clusters —
Ad Grant compliance tools (GrantMax, agencies) that don't know what FCRA is, and
GEO/AI-visibility tools (GEO Visibility, RankMac) that don't know what a Google

product.** That gap is real — narrower than "no competitors," which is the
honest and more defensible claim to build a pitch on.

## 2. Why "free SEO/GEO audit for NGOs" is not a wedge

RankMac already runs this exact funnel — free audit, NGO-targeted copy, tiered
pricing in the same ₹9k–₹35k/month band originally proposed for SEctOr. GEO
Visibility already runs the AI-citation-tracking version. **Leading with "we
audit your SEO" competes on a feature that's already commoditized and already
has agency sales teams behind it.**

## 3. Why "Ad Grants management" alone is not a wedge either

GrantMax and a real agency ecosystem already own "audit and fix your Ad Grants
account." A self-serve tool competing purely on Ad Grant compliance is entering
a category with an established incumbent and an established buying behavior
("give an agency access") that's easier to sell against software than software
is to sell against it.

---

## 4. The actual wedge: the intersection, not either piece alone

```
Ad Grant compliance tools    ∩    GEO/SEO visibility tools    ∩    India NGO eligibility logic
   (GrantMax, agencies)              (GEO Visibility, RankMac)         (nobody confirmed)
```

The wedge isn't "better SEO audit" or "better Ad Grant audit." It's the
question none of the confirmed competitors answer in one place:

> **"Is my NGO even eligible for the ₹8L/month Google gives away, and if so, is
> my website good enough to make use of it?"**

That's a compound question — eligibility (FCRA-gated, verified against Google's
current rules) + capacity (Ad Grant utilization) + readiness (SEO/GEO score) —
and answering it requires exactly the three pieces no single confirmed
competitor has stitched together.

---

## 5. Recommended USP framing

**Category:** NGO Visibility & Grant Readiness Intelligence — not "SEO tool,"
not "Ad Grants tool." Naming it either commoditizes it against a real,
confirmed incumbent in that specific category.

**ICP (narrowed by the WTP research, not aspirational):** FCRA-registered
Indian NGOs — Society, Trust, or Section 8 — with enough organizational
maturity to have a real discretionary software budget. Not the median Indian
NGO (whose entire annual IT spend can be under ₹30,000 using free/discounted
Google Workspace and Zoho tiers) — the professionally staffed, foundation- or
CSR-backed segment above that.

**Core promise:**
> "Know whether your NGO is eligible, visible, compliant, and making the most
> of its Google Ad Grant — in one place, verified against Google's actual
> current rules, not assumptions."

**Primary wedge:** Ad Grant intelligence — concrete, ₹-denominated, easy to
justify a price against. "Improve your SEO" has vague economic value and is
hard to price at ₹15k/month. "Your eligible account has ₹X/month of unused
capacity" is not.

**Supporting layer:** SEO/GEO diagnostics — explains *why* the NGO isn't
converting the ad spend it could have (thin landing pages, no schema, no FAQ
structure), which is what makes the Ad Grant number actionable rather than
just alarming.

**Retention mechanism:** weekly monitoring + prioritized actions — the thing
that turns a one-time audit into a reason to keep paying.

**Long-term moat — the one thing no competitor above can copy in a sprint:**
a longitudinal benchmark dataset. GrantMax can clone an Ad Grants compliance
checklist by next quarter. It cannot clone 12–18 months of "here's how similar
Indian NGOs typically score" data, because that data only exists after real
volume runs through the product. **The wedge gets someone in the door. The
benchmark is why they don't leave for a cheaper clone later.**

## 6. What NOT to say, now that competitors are confirmed real

| Don't say | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Nobody's built Ad Grant compliance software" | False — GrantMax exists | "The tools that check Ad Grant compliance don't know what FCRA is" |
| "Nobody tracks AI/GEO visibility" | False — GEO Visibility exists | "The tools that track AI visibility don't know what a Google nonprofit grant is" |
| "We're the only NGO SEO tool" | False — RankMac markets directly to NGOs | "We're the only one that connects visibility to a specific, claimable ₹ opportunity" |

Every corrected claim above is a stronger pitch than the false version it
replaces — specific and checkable beats broad and deniable, especially to a
buyer (an NGO director, a CSR team) who will absolutely Google the competing
tools before signing anything.

## 7. Before this goes on a landing page

- **Rename before public launch.** `sectorapp.in` is a live, unrelated, early-access
  product right now. Run an actual IP India Class 9/42 trademark search before
  committing to a final name — don't let the name collision get discovered after
  copy, code, and outreach lists already say "SEctOr."
- **Don't claim category exclusivity in outreach copy.** Say the narrow true
  thing (the intersection nobody's built) — it survives a skeptical NGO director
  Googling "Google Ad Grants audit tool" and landing on GrantMax's homepage
  mid-sales-call, which the broad claim does not.
